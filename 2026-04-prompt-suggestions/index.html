Prompt Suggestions—
writing instructions an LLM can actually follow.

Prompting remains important with LLMs because it is the main way you translate your intent into instructions the model can actually follow. The quality of your instructions determines the quality, safety, and usefulness of the output.

2 Tips 1 Worked Example 2 Videos ≈ 6 min read
01
Tip One

Be specific.

LLMs respond best to clear instructions. The more context you give, the better the output. Vague prompts produce generic emails. Specific prompts produce emails you can send.

Scenario: a follow-up email after meeting a candidate at Safety Fest Tennessee.

Take 1 — Slightly Vague Generic
"Draft a follow-up email to the safety conference to the people I met in the booth that promote our Bachelor of Science degree in Fire Science to someone who has an AA degree in Fire Science."
SubjectGreat Meeting You at the Safety Conference To[First Name]

Hi [First Name],

It was great meeting you at the conference and having the opportunity to talk about your background in Fire Science and your career goals.

At Columbia Southern University, we work with many fire service professionals who already hold an Associate of Arts or Associate of Applied Science degree in Fire Science…

Our Bachelor of Science in Fire Science program is designed to support working professionals by offering:

  • 100% online coursework
  • Flexible scheduling
  • Generous transfer credit opportunities
  • Curriculum focused on leadership, operations, safety, and emergency services management

If you would like, I would be happy to: answer questions about transfer credits, review how your associate degree may apply, provide tuition information, connect you with an admissions representative…

Best regards,
[Your Name] · [Title]
Columbia Southern University

Take 2 — More Specific Sendable
"Write a 2-paragraph follow-up email to Alex Rivera, a Firefighter/Engineer with Example Fire Department. We met at the booth and talked about advancing his career. He has an AA in Fire Science from Chattanooga State and is a certified EMT, HAZMAT technician, and Confined Space instructor.

Recommend the B.S. in Fire Administration, Fire Investigation concentration as his next step. Mention that it's FESHE-recognized, that his AA transfers in for up to 90 credits, and that the concentration complements his HAZMAT background.

Tone: warm and professional, not salesy. End with an invitation to schedule a 15-minute call about transfer credits."
SubjectGreat Meeting You at the Conference ToAlex Rivera · Example Fire Dept.

Hi Alex,

It was a pleasure meeting you at the conference and learning more about your experience with Example Fire Department. Your background stood out right away — the combination of your Fire Science education from Chattanooga State along with your EMT certification, HAZMAT technician experience, and work as a Confined Space instructor. Based on our conversation, I think Columbia Southern's B.S. in Fire Administration with the Fire Investigation concentration would be an excellent next step. The program is FESHE-recognized, and your associate degree may allow you to transfer in up to 90 credits toward completion. The concentration also aligns naturally with your HAZMAT and technical-response background.

One of the things many firefighters appreciate is the flexibility to keep working while completing the degree online. The curriculum is built around leadership, administration, investigations, and operational decision-making. If you'd like, I'd be happy to schedule a quick 15-minute call to review transfer credits and answer any questions about the program or timeline. I enjoyed our conversation and hope we can stay in touch.

Best regards,
[Your Name] · [Title]
Columbia Southern University

What changed?

The strong prompt answers six questions the vague one doesn't.

  • Who is the recipient?
  • What's the context of our conversation?
  • What background personalizes the email?
  • What's the recommendation?
  • What tone and call to action?
  • What format or length is needed?
Rule of ThumbHeuristic

If you wouldn't hand the prompt to a new hire or teammate and expect them to write the email correctly, the AI can't either.

02
Tip Two

Ask AI to help improve your prompts.

Iterate. Don't abandon a response if it's not perfect — refine the prompt or ask for a revision. Quality input gives you quality output.

2–3 drafts
For small, day-to-day tasks
8–10 drafts
For complicated or high-stakes prompts

Useful follow-ups to keep on hand

“

Help me improve my last prompt.

“

Revise this to one page between 275–300 words.

“

Revise this to one paragraph between 100–125 words.

“

Rewrite using plain language at a 7th-grade reading level.

Rule of ThumbWord Count

For cleaner executive-style formatting with headings, bullets, and white space, assume 300–375 words per page.
