Step by step

Turn your phone into a mobile thinking partner.

Much of our work happens in conversation: at commencement, at an alumni gathering, on a conference floor, or across the table from a potential learning partner. Voice lets you capture what you learn, notice what your week is telling you, and reflect on it with AI while the details are fresh.

01
Get ready

Start in a few minutes.

You do not need to be an AI expert, and you do not need to try everything here. Pick one small experiment this week.

  1. Choose the app and account for your use. Use the official mobile app from OpenAI, Anthropic, or Microsoft in your phone’s app store, where permitted.
  2. Sign in, open a chat, and start voice. In ChatGPT, select the Voice icon in the message bar. In Claude, select the sound wave beside the microphone. In consumer Copilot, select the microphone in the message composer. Work versions of Copilot may look different.1–4
  3. Allow microphone access when prompted. Try a quiet spot, have a short conversation, end the voice session, and review what was saved. Features and limits vary by account and app version.
Try saying

I’m trying voice for the first time. Ask me one question about my day, then help me turn my answer into a short note.

Voice conversation and dictation differ. Voice lets you talk back and forth; dictation turns speech into text. Either helps. Test with something ordinary before you use real work details.

02
Capture

Capture a conversation while it is fresh.

Two ways to do it, then ideas for each team.

Option one: talk through a recap afterward

After an interaction, take a minute or two to describe what you learned. Include the person’s name only when the approved tool allows it, the details that mattered, and any next step you agreed to. Spell names and check dates. Keep your impressions separate from what the person said.

Try saying

Help me organize this recap into key details, what mattered to the person, and agreed next steps. Separate what I heard from my impressions. Ask about anything unclear, and do not fill in missing facts.

Option two: invite AI into the conversation

Start with a willing colleague in a quiet place. Before you turn on voice, explain that the app will process the conversation and may save it. Ask everyone involved for permission, use an approved tool, and make declining easy.

A way to ask

Would you be comfortable if I use my AI assistant to help capture our conversation and, when we invite it, suggest a follow-up question? The app processes what we say and may save the conversation. We can leave it off if you prefer.

Treat this as a short, shared experiment. Voice chat may respond during pauses, and it is not a guaranteed silent recorder or complete transcript. Invite a question at a natural break, review the summary together, correct anything missed, and stop the session when you finish.

What to capture

Alumni Relations

Capture alumni stories

An alumnus’s story, a moment that mattered, or an idea for a future gathering. Ask separately before using their story publicly.

Corporate Outreach

Organize conference insights

The program of interest, open questions, and the person’s own start timeframe: one to three months, six months or longer, or undecided. Do not let AI infer readiness from tone.

Business Development

Strengthen partnership follow-up

The organization’s learning needs, interest in a partnership for employees and family members, and the agreed follow-up. Verify program and discount details before sharing them.

03
Notice

Journal by voice in two projects.

A few minutes, daily or two to three times a week, is enough to start.

  1. Use two projects to store your reflections. Create one called “Joy and Success” and one called “Just FIXIT!” and keep each entry in the matching project. If your app does not offer Projects, or does not support voice inside them, use two clearly named sets of dated chats or two saved notes.
  2. Journal daily, or two to three times a week. Use dated entries, and keep personal reflections separate from work records.

Joy and Success (five minutes a day)

Talk about what went well, what felt successful, and what brought you joy or happiness. What helped those moments happen? Five minutes is plenty; take up to ten if you want. On a difficult day, there is no need to manufacture a positive moment.

Try saying

I want to add a Joy and Success entry for today. Ask me what went well, what felt successful, and what brought me joy, one question at a time. When we finish, give me a short dated summary I can keep.

Just FIXIT! (no more than five minutes a day)

Describe what drained your energy or made the day harder. Perhaps it was expense reporting, repeated data entry, or another tedious task. What part caused the friction, and does it keep happening? Focus on the task and process, and leave out unnecessary personal details about other people.

Try saying

I want to add a Just FIXIT! entry for today. Ask me what drained my energy or made the day harder, and which part of the task caused the friction, one question at a time. When we finish, give me a short dated summary I can keep.

Test the setup with ordinary details before you add anything sensitive.

04
Reflect

Review the week on Friday.

After Friday’s entry, start a new chat in each project and ask AI to reflect the week back to you.

  1. Start a new chat in each project once Friday’s entry is done.
  2. Ask which dates AI can see. Do not assume a project name or memory feature gives it access to every entry. If any dates are missing, paste them in before you ask for patterns.
  3. Talk it through as you would with a thought partner. Treat its themes as possibilities, and ask it to point to specific entries.

Joy and Success

Try saying

I’ve been journaling about what went well this week and want to think it through with you. What keeps showing up? Where did I light up? Point to specific entries, and end with one question that helps me see what I might be missing.

Just FIXIT!

A process problem may need a process change. The aim is to understand what would help, not to accept unnecessary frustration.

Try saying

I’ve been journaling about what got in my way this week. What keeps showing up? Which sticking points could be simplified, automated, delegated, or improved with AI, and where is AI not a good fit? Help me pick one small change to test next week.

Your invitation for this week

Try one conversation exercise and choose one project to begin. On Friday, ask yourself: What did voice help me capture? What needed correcting? What is one thing I want to keep doing?
